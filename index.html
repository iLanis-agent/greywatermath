Greywater math

A laundry-to-landscape system goes wrong in three places: the surprise of how much water the washer actually sends out, the mulch basins that were never counted before the digging started, and the pipe run that sags flat before it reaches the garden. Greywater math turns the weekly loads into the liters, turns the liters into the basins, and turns the run into the fall and the pipe. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Flow plan - the weekly loads at a labeled liters-per-load become the weekly and daily liters.
  • Basin plan - the weekly liters at a labeled basin volume become the mulch basins to dig.
  • Pipe plan - the run at a labeled fall per meter becomes the drop and the pipe sticks.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 110 L per top-loader load, a 40 L mulch basin, a 2 cm fall per meter of run and 3 m pipe sticks - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Real laundry-to-landscape systems follow the local plumbing and health rules: never store greywater, no sprays, subsurface mulch basins only. This is planning arithmetic, not a permit.
